# IBPS PO — exam overview and strategy

The Probationary Officer (PO) Common Recruitment Process is run by the **Institute of
Banking Personnel Selection (IBPS)** for probationary officer and management trainee
posts across participating public sector banks. Everything on this page follows the
**CRP-PO/MT-XVI** notification.

> **IBPS revises the pattern.** The question counts, marks and timings have changed
> more than once. The figures below are the latest published ones — and this section
> carries a "last verified" stamp that a scheduled job refreshes. If the stamp is old,
> confirm against [ibps.in](https://www.ibps.in/) before you plan your final month.

---

## The four stages

| Stage | What it is | Marks | Qualifying? |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Prelims** | Online objective test, 60 minutes | 100 | Yes — sectional **and** overall cut-off |
| **Mains** | Online objective test, 160 minutes | 200 | Yes — sectional **and** overall cut-off |
| **Descriptive** | Essay + comprehension, 30 minutes | 25 | Yes — must attempt |
| **Personality Test** | Self-report, proctored or at a centre | — | **Non-qualifying, but attendance is mandatory** |
| **Interview** | Conducted by the participating bank | 100 | Yes |

**Final merit = Mains marks + Interview marks**, weighted **80 : 20**. Prelims marks are
**not** carried into the final merit — they only decide who reaches Mains.

**The Personality Test is non-qualifying but you must attend it.** Skipping it makes you
ineligible for the interview, even if your Mains score is high. Treat it as a mandatory
appointment.

## Prelims

**100 questions · 100 marks · 60 minutes** — 20 minutes per section, separately timed.

| Section | Questions | Marks | Time | Medium |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| English Language | 30 | 30 | 20 min | English |
| Quantitative Aptitude | 35 | 30 | 20 min | English and Hindi |
| Reasoning Ability | 35 | 40 | 20 min | English and Hindi |
| **Total** | **100** | **100** | **60 min** | |

**Three things people get wrong:**

1. **The marks are not equal to the question count.** Reasoning is 35 questions but
   **40 marks**; Quant is 35 questions but only **30 marks**. That is a deliberate
   rebalancing — Reasoning is now the highest-scoring section in Prelims.
2. **Sections are separately timed.** There is no switching between sections, and you
   cannot carry unused minutes forward. If you are stuck, guess and move on.
3. **Negative marking is 0.25 per wrong answer** in both papers. A blank costs nothing.

**Attempting advice.** With 20 minutes and 35 questions, you get ~34 seconds per
question. That is enough for Quant arithmetic and Syllogism, and **not enough for
puzzles**. Budget 8–10 minutes per puzzle set, and do at most two in Reasoning.

## Mains

**170 questions · 200 marks · 160 minutes** — separately timed sections.

| Section | Questions | Marks | Time | Medium |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Reasoning | 40 | 60 | 45 min | English and Hindi |
| General / Economy / Banking Awareness / Digital & Financial Awareness (incl. RBI circulars) | 50 | 60 | 35 min | English and Hindi |
| English Language | 40 | 20 | 35 min | English |
| Data Analysis & Interpretation | 40 | 60 | 45 min | English and Hindi |
| **Objective total** | **170** | **200** | **160 min** | |

**Then the Descriptive paper:**

| Paper | Questions | Marks | Time | Medium |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Essay and Comprehension | 2 | 25 | 30 min | English |

**What changed and why it matters.**

- The objective paper grew from 145 to **170 questions**, while marks and time stayed
  at 200 and 160 minutes. **Each question is worth less** — roughly 1.18 marks on
  average. Attempting more but scoring less per question means **breadth beats depth**.
- **English marks fell from 40 to 20** while the question count rose to 40. English is
  now the cheapest section to score in Mains: 40 questions for 20 marks, and the
  questions are mostly RC and grammar.
- **DI is a separate 40-question section worth 60 marks**, and General/Economy/Banking
  carries 50 questions for 60 marks — the two heaviest sections by marks-per-minute.
- **Computer Aptitude is no longer a separate section.** It was folded into Reasoning
  and into the Digital/Financial Awareness part of General Awareness. Do not skip it —
  it is now hiding in two places.

## Negative marking, in one line

**−0.25 for every wrong objective answer. Zero for a blank.**

Do the maths on it. In Prelims, if you answer 60 questions and get 35 right:

```
35 correct  →  35 marks
25 wrong    →  −6.25
Net         =  28.75
```

Answering only the 35 you are sure of gives you **35**. That is a 22% swing for the
same amount of reading. **When you are below 50% sure, leave it blank.**

## The cut-off reality

There are two cut-offs per paper: **sectional** and **overall**.

- **Sectional** is what actually bites. Scoring 60 overall but 8 in a section usually
  means you are out, whatever the total.
- **Overall** scales with the number of vacancies and how many people apply.
- **Mains cut-offs are much higher than Prelims**, and the **General/Economy/Banking**
  section is usually the highest cut-off of all — because it is the easiest to prepare
  for and therefore the most competitive.

**Plan for the section you are weakest in.** If Quant is your weak section, it caps your
overall score no matter how well you do elsewhere. Fix your weakest section first.

---

## The language of the paper

- **Prelims and Mains** are offered in **English and Hindi**, except the English
  Language section, which is **English only**.
- **The descriptive paper is English only.**
- Reasoning questions are frequently **coded** — symbols instead of words. Read the
  coding key carefully; it is a free source of marks if you do, and a total loss if you
  do not.

## A note on current affairs

Current affairs is **not** a subject you can revise; it is a habit. The 15–30 questions
in Mains General Awareness are mostly from the **last 5–6 months**. The system that
works:

- Read one newspaper daily — *The Hindu* or *Indian Express* business page, plus
  *Business Standard* or *Mint* for economy.
- Maintain a **single-page monthly tracker**: policy announcements, committee
  appointments, government schemes, awards, sports results, important days.
- Revise the tracker **weekly** and the whole thing **monthly**.
- Pair it with a **static GK** base: RBI history, five-year plans, committees, firsts in
  banking and economy.

Do not collect newspaper clippings. The tracker is the whole system — one line per item,
readable in five minutes.
