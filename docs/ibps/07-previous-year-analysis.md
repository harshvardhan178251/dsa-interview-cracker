# IBPS PO — previous-year analysis and scoring strategy

**The purpose of this note:** to stop you from preparing uniformly across a syllabus that
is not uniformly weighted, and to stop you from treating a mock as a test rather than a
diagnostic.

---

## Where the marks actually are

### Prelims (100 marks, 60 minutes)

| Section | Questions | Marks | The high-weight block | Share of the section |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| **Reasoning** | 35 | 40 | Puzzles & seating | 55–65% |
| **Quant** | 35 | 30 | DI + arithmetic | 60–70% |
| **English** | 30 | 30 | RC + cloze + error spotting | 60–70% |

**Read that table again.** Reasoning carries **40 of the 100 marks** even though it has
fewer questions than Quant. And within Reasoning, puzzles alone are 18–25 questions.

**The four pillars** — puzzles, DI, reading comprehension, and current affairs — are more
than half of the scoreable marks across both papers.

### Mains (200 marks + 25 descriptive)

| Section | Questions | Marks | Time | Marks per minute |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Reasoning | 40 | 60 | 45 min | 1.33 |
| General/Economy/Banking | 50 | 60 | 35 min | **1.71** |
| English | 40 | 20 | 35 min | **0.57** |
| Data Analysis & Interpretation | 40 | 60 | 45 min | 1.33 |

**The "marks per minute" column is the whole strategy for Mains.**

- **General/Economy/Banking is the most efficient section** — 1.71 marks per minute.
  It is also the most competitive. It deserves the most preparation per hour.
- **English is the least efficient** — 0.57 marks per minute, but it is also the
  **easiest** section to prepare for and the easiest to score in. Forty questions for
  20 marks means you can leave it partly unattempted and still not have wasted much time.
- **Reasoning and DI are in the middle** — 1.33 marks per minute, but both need
  practice, so they take more *learning* time even though they pay well per minute in
  the exam.

### Where to put your hours

For someone starting from zero, the recommended split is:

| Section | Share of study hours | Why |
|---|---:|---|
| **Quant + DI** | 30% | The most concepts, the widest gap from zero |
| **Reasoning** | 25% | Puzzles need volume, not just method |
| **English** | 20% | The fastest return, but it plateaus |
| **Banking + Economy** | 15% | A lot to memorise, so it must start early |
| **Computer aptitude** | 5% | A few days of work for 8–10 marks |
| **Current affairs** | 5% | But it runs **daily** in parallel, forever |

**Current affairs gets 5% of your revision hours but 100% of your daily habits.** That
asymmetry is the point.

## The attempt strategy

### Prelims — 20 minutes per section

**The rule: never let a section eat the next one.** With separate timers, a section you
overrun is a section you lose entirely.

**A workable plan for each 20-minute section:**

| Minutes | What |
|---:|---|
| 0–2 | Read all the questions. Mark the ones you can answer immediately |
| 2–13 | Answer those. Do not touch a puzzle yet |
| 13–19 | One puzzle at most, only if you have already banked the easy marks |
| 19–20 | Mark the rest for review. Move on |

**Target scores in Prelims:**

| Section | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Reasoning | 20–24 / 35 | One puzzle solved accurately plus the small questions |
| Quant | 20–24 / 35 | DI (10–12) + simplification/series (7–9) + arithmetic (5–7) |
| English | 18–24 / 30 | RC (6–8) + cloze (5–6) + error spotting (5–6) + fillers (3–4) |

**A safe Prelims total is around 55–65.** That is normally enough to clear the sectional
cut-offs and reach Mains. You do not need 80 to qualify — you need to clear **three
sectional cut-offs**, not a total.

### Mains — 45 minutes for Reasoning and DI

| Section | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Reasoning (40 Q, 45 min)** | 1 puzzle (12 min) + the small questions (25 min) + review (8 min) |
| **General/Economy/Banking (50 Q, 35 min)** | First pass all 50 in 20 min (banking + static GK + economy), second pass current affairs in 10 min |
| **English (40 Q, 35 min)** | RC first (they are the most marks per question), then cloze, then error spotting, then fillers |
| **DI (40 Q, 45 min)** | 2 full sets (caselet + table/line), then the single-question DIs |

**The General/Economy/Banking plan is the single most efficient habit in the exam.**
Most candidates attempt it last, when they are tired, and score poorly on the section
with the highest cut-off. **Do it second, right after Reasoning, while you are sharp.**

### The descriptive paper — 30 minutes

**Attempt both questions.** A blank essay is 0; a structured, mediocre essay is 12+. The
descriptive paper is often a **qualifying** component, so a blank can cost you
everything.

Plan: 3 minutes planning, 12 writing, 3 checking per question.

## Reading cut-offs

**Two cut-offs: sectional and overall.** Both must be cleared.

**What determines them:**
- **Number of vacancies** — fewer vacancies, higher cut-offs.
- **Number of applicants** — a large applicant pool raises them.
- **The difficulty of that year's paper** — an unexpectedly hard paper lowers them.

**How to read them practically:**

1. **Compare with your own mock score, not with other candidates' claims.** Cut-off
   discussions on forums are noise.
2. **Your sectional score is what matters.** If Quant cut-off is 14 and you scored 13 in
   Quant and 30 in English, you are out — whatever your total.
3. **Track the trend, not the number.** Cut-offs move a few marks year to year. If the
   sectional cut-off for Quant has been 14, 15, 13, 16 — plan for 16.
4. **Target above the previous year's sectional cut-off by 3–4 marks**, because the
   number of applicants grows every cycle.

**The one number to watch:** in Mains, **General/Economy/Banking Awareness** almost
always has the highest sectional cut-off, because it is the most accessible. A
candidate who ignores GA because "it is just current affairs" fails the Mains.

## Analysing a mock — the part that actually teaches you

**A mock you did not analyse was a mock you failed.** This is the single highest-leverage
habit in bank exam prep, and almost nobody does it.

### The 45-minute analysis routine

Run it immediately after the mock, before you look at anything else.

| Step | Minutes | What |
|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 5 | Note your section-wise score and, more importantly, your **section-wise time** |
| 2 | 10 | For every question you got **wrong**, write the reason in one line, using the categories below |
| 3 | 10 | For every question you got **right but slowly**, note the time — slow and right is still a risk |
| 4 | 10 | For every question you **skipped**, decide: could I have got it with more time, or is it beyond me? |
| 5 | 5 | Write down the **three** things you will fix before the next mock |
| 6 | 5 | The questions you could not solve at all — these go into a "learn this" list |

### The reason categories

Tag every wrong answer with one of these. The distribution tells you where to spend the
next week.

| Tag | Meaning | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Concept gap** | You did not know the method | Re-read the topic note, then drill |
| **Careless** | You knew it and slipped | Slow down on this question type; re-do it a week later |
| **Ran out of time** | You knew it but the clock beat you | Practise this topic type under a timer |
| **Misread** | You understood a different question | Read the last line of the question before solving |
| **Guessed** | You marked an option at random | Add it to the "solvable" list; it was worth 0.75 |
| **Beyond me** | Genuinely new | Learn it, and count how many times it appears |

**If more than a third of your errors are "beyond me", you are revising the wrong
topics.** If more than a third are "careless", your revision is fine and your *accuracy*
under time pressure needs work.

### The three-things rule

After every mock, write down exactly **three** things to fix. Not ten. Three.

Ten things means you fix none of them. Three is a workload you will actually complete,
and after four mocks you will have fixed twelve specific issues — which is worth more
than a vague sense that "quant is weak".

### Track it in a simple table

```
Mock #   Date    Eng  Quant  Reasoning   Marks   3 things to fix
 1      12 Oct   14     11       16         41    DI ratios; SVA rules; 3 puzzles too slow
 2      19 Oct   17     15       19         51    DI caselet base; RC inference; finish English
```

**Six columns, five minutes to fill in.** After six mocks this table tells you more
about your preparation than any amount of self-assessment.

## Common self-assessment mistakes

| Mistake | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| Counting a mock you did not analyse | A raw score measures your mood as much as your prep |
| Comparing your score to other candidates' | Their mocks differ in difficulty and their cut-offs are fiction |
| Treating 60/100 as "failure" | The Prelims sectional cut-offs are usually 12–18 per section; 60 total with balanced sections clears comfortably |
| Revising only what you got wrong | The questions you skipped were worth points too |
| Not tracking time per section | You cannot fix a pace problem you never measured |
| Practising full mocks only | You never get the reps; practise topic-wise sets too |
| One mock a week | Too slow to find a pattern. Two or three a week, once you are past week 6 |

## The last 30 days

| Weeks | What |
|---|---|
| **−4 to −3** | Finish the syllabus. No mocks yet, or one to calibrate |
| **−2** | Topic-wise speed drills. Two full mocks. Start the formula-sheet revision ritual |
| **−1** | Three to four full mocks, each analysed the same day. Revise the formula sheet daily |
| **−7 days** | Two mocks, then **stop**. Only the formula sheet and the current-affairs tracker |
| **−2 days** | Formula sheet, banking facts, and the last 30 days of current affairs. **No new topics** |
| **−1 day** | Nothing. Sleep well. Set out your documents |

**The most common last-week mistake** is starting a new topic. It feels productive and
it destroys a week of consolidation. If you have not covered something by then, you
will not cover it in seven days — and you will have lost the confidence that comes from
being well prepared.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Where are the marks? | Puzzles, DI, RC, current affairs — more than half of everything |
| What is the safest Prelims score? | 55–65, with all three sectionals cleared |

| Which Mains section is cheapest to score? | English — 40 questions for 20 marks |
| Which section should I do second? | General/Economy/Banking, while you are sharp |
