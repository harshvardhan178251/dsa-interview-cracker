# IBPS PO — Banking, Economy and Current Affairs

**Mains:** 50 questions · 60 marks · 35 minutes
**Level:** basic → intermediate, with a rolling current-affairs component

This is the section with the **most marks and the shortest time** in the Mains paper: 60
marks in 35 minutes. And it is the section with the **highest cut-off**, because it is
the easiest to prepare for and therefore the most crowded.

**The paradox to plan around:** it is simultaneously the most valuable section (60 marks)
and the most competitive (highest cut-off). You cannot avoid it.

## Layer 1 — banking awareness

### Account types

| Account | Who can open | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Savings** | Individuals | 4–5 free transactions per month; ~3.25–4% on balance for small balances |
| **Current** | Businesses | No limit; overdraft facility; higher interest than savings |
| **Fixed / Recurring Deposit** | All | Term deposits, higher rates, premature encashment penalty |
| **NRE / NRO / FCNR** | Non-residents | NRE tax-free; NRO taxable; FCNR in foreign currency |
| **CASA** | — | Current + Savings; the cheapest deposits for a bank |

**Savings account rules to memorise:**
- Minimum balance: ₹0 to ₹2,500 (depending on the bank; SBI requires ₹1,000 for basic,
  ₹5,000 for Prime, ₹10,000 for Advantage).
- Free ATM withdrawals: **8 in metro cities, 6 in other cities** per month (RBI 2022
  revision; earlier it was 10/6 — check the circular).
- Free UPI transactions: **24 hours** (RBI raised this from 10 in January 2021).
- Unclaimed deposits go to **DICGC** (Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation).

### Cards — see the [computer aptitude note](./04-computer-apptitude.md) for the full card
breakdown and the interest rule.

### Loans

| Loan | Against | Rate driver | Key fact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Home loan** | Property | Repo rate | 80–90% LTV; tax benefit under Sec 80EEA (self-occupied, ₹2 lakh) |
| **Auto / Personal** | Asset / Income | BPLR | |

| **Mortgage loan** | Property, kept | | Lower rate than a personal loan |
| **Gold loan** | Gold jewellery | | Quick; usually 75–80% of value |
| **Loan against FD / PPF** | Your deposit | | |
| **DSA / MUDRA** | — | | MUDRA: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) |
| **CAB (Customs Arrangement for Deemed Exporters)** | Export | | For exporters |

**Key RBI terms:**
- **Repo** — RBI lends to banks against government securities. **Reverse repo** — banks lend
  to RBI.
- **Bank Rate** — the rate at which RBI lends long-term funds to banks.
- **MSF / Marginal Standing Facility** — banks borrow from RBI overnight against SLR
  securities.
- **CRR** — Cash Reserve Ratio, held with RBI, **not** remunerative.
- **SLR** — Statutory Liquidity Ratio, held in **gold, government securities or cash**,
  **remunerative**.
- **LCR / NSFR** — Liquidity Coverage Ratio, Net Stable Funding Ratio (Basel III).

### The RBI — functions

The Reserve Bank of India was established on **1 April 1935** under the **RBI Act 1934**
and nationalised in **1949**. Its headquarters are in **Mumbai**. The current Governor is
**Sanjay Malhotra** (took office in December 2024).

**Functions:**
1. **Monetary authority** — issues currency, controls inflation, repo/reverse repo.
2. **Issuer of currency** — the **only** body that issues banknotes in India (coins are
   issued by the Government of India, minted by the RBI).
3. **Banker to the Government** — banker and debt manager for the central and state
   governments.
4. **Banker's to banks** — holds their reserves, lender of last resort.
5. **Manager of foreign exchange** — manages the forex reserves and the rupee's value.
6. **Controller of credit** — credit policy.
7. **Supervisor of banks and financial institutions** (added by the 1934 Act amendment in
   1949).

**RBI's assets and liabilities** — on the liabilities side: **CRR, other liabilities,
RBI's own capital**. On the asset side: **gold, foreign securities, Government
securities, loans to governments, other assets**.

**Inflation measures:**
- **CPI (Consumer Price Index)** — by the **National Statistical Office (NSO)**, now
  under the **Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI)**. CPI is the
  target of the MPC's inflation objective (4% with a 2% band).
- **WPI (Wholesale Price Index)** — by the **Office of the Economic Adviser** under the
  **Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT)**.

### Monetary policy

The **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** has **6 members**: 3 from the RBI (the Governor
chairs it), and 3 appointed by the Central Government. It meets **bi-monthly** (six
times a year, roughly every six weeks).

**The MPC's mandate (since 2016):** keep **CPI inflation** at **4%** with a tolerance
band of **±2%**. If inflation is above 6% or below 2% for three consecutive quarters,
the RBI must report to the Centre.

**Policy rates:**
- **Repo rate** — the rate at which the RBI lends to banks.
- **Reverse repo** — the rate at which banks lend to the RBI. The **liquidity surplus
  is absorbed by raising the reverse repo** and the **deficit is funded by lowering the
  repo**.
- **Bank rate** — long-term lending to banks.
- **MSF** — the marginal overnight facility.
- **CRR** — the RBI can raise it to mop up liquidity.
- **OMO (Open Market Operations)** — buying or selling government securities.
- **MDR (Marginal Standing Rate)**, **SDF (Standing Deposit Facility)** — added in 2022
  for flexible liquidity management.

**Corridor:** the MPC's rate sits between the **MSF (ceiling)** and the **SDF (floor)**.

**Transmission:** repo → banks' cost of funds → lending rates → deposit rates → the
general price level.

| Body | Role |
|---|---|

| **SEBI** | Securities and Exchange Board of India — capital markets regulator |
| **IRDAI** | Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India |
| **PFRDA** | Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority |
| **NABARD** | National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development — rural credit |
| **NHB** | National Housing Bank — housing finance |
| **SIDBI** | Small Industries Development Bank of India — MSME finance |
| **EXIM Bank** | Export-Import Bank — international trade finance |
| **NABARD**, **SIDBI**, **EXIM** | All are **development finance institutions (DFIs)** |
| **FCRA** | Financial Crimes Redirection Authority — PMLA |

| **SFIO** | Serious Fraud Investigation Office |

**Deposit insurance:** **DICGC** insures deposits up to **₹5 lakh** per depositor per
bank. It is a statutory body under the DICGC Act 1961.

**Insurance types:** Life (LIC), General (health, motor, property — IRDAI regulates),
Reinsurance.

**Capital markets:**
- **SEBI was formed in 1988** (statutory body; the earlier SEBI was a non-statutory
  board in 1988, made statutory by the SEBI Act 1992).
- **Depository:** NSDL (since 1988) and CDSL (since 1992).
- **Stock exchanges:** BSE (1994, Asia's oldest), NSE (1994).
- **Mutual funds:** AMFI; **SIP**; **ELSS** has a 3-year lock-in and a 40% exemption under
  Section 80C.
- **Primary and secondary markets.**
- **Demutualisation** of exchanges — NSE was the first in India.

**Insurance terms:**
- **Premium**, **sum assured**, **maturity value**, **grace period**, **waiting period**,
  **free-look period** (14–30 days), **underwriting**, **reinsurance**, **IRDA**.

## Layer 2 — Indian economy

### Basic concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **GDP** | Market value of all final goods and services produced in a country in a year |
| **GNP** | GDP + net factor income from abroad |
| **NDP** | GDP − depreciation |
| **Per capita income** | GDP ÷ population |
| **Inflation** | Sustained rise in the general price level |
| **Deflation** | Sustained fall — dangerous because debt burdens rise |
| **Stagflation** | Stagnant growth + high inflation |
| **Recession** | Two consecutive quarters of negative GDP growth |
| **Fiscal deficit** | Government's total expenditure − total receipts |
| **Revenue deficit** | Revenue expenditure − revenue receipts |
| **Primary deficit** | Fiscal deficit − interest payments |
| **Balance of payments** | Current account + capital account |
| **FDI vs FPI** | FDI = direct ownership; FPI = portfolio investment |
| **Repo, CRR, SLR, MSB, Monetary policy** | see the banking layer above |

**The GDP income and expenditure methods:**
- **Income**: GDP = wages + rent + interest + profit.
- **Expenditure**: GDP = C + I + G + (X − M).
- **Production**: value added at each stage to avoid double counting.

**Inflation measures:** CPI (retail, NSO/MoSPI, the MPC's target) and WPI (wholesale,
DPIIT).

**Types of inflation:** demand-pull, cost-push, built-in, seasonal, monetary.

### Monetary and fiscal policy

| | Monetary policy (RBI) | Fiscal policy (Government) |
|---|---|---|
| Controls | Money supply, interest rates | Government spending and taxation |
| Tool | Repo, CRR, OMO, MSF | Budget, GST, fiscal deficit |
| Aim | Price stability | Growth + employment + equity |

**The 2023-24 statutory status of the RBI:** the **RBI Act was amended in 2023** to give
the RBI a framework for conducting **monetary policy in inflation target mode**, with the
MPC explicitly in charge. That is a favourite question.

### The Union Budget and Economic Survey

- The **Union Budget** is presented on **1 February** (the Finance Bill is presented;
  the accounts are on 2 February).
- The **Economic Survey** is presented on **31 January**.
- The Finance Minister presents the Budget.
- The **FRBM Act 2003** gave the government a framework for a fiscal deficit target;
  the **FRBM Review Committee** (N.K. Singh committee, 2014) recommended 3% of GDP,
  and the **N.K. Singh Committee (2018)** on the FRBM Act recommended 2.5–3%.
- The NKR (Nirmala Sitharaman) Committee recommended the **Fiscal Responsibility and
  Budget Management (FRBM) Act, 2003** — and the **N.K. Singh** committee on fiscal
  consolidation.
- The **Fiscal Policy Statements** include: the Medium-Term Fiscal Policy Statement, the
  Fiscal Policy Strategy Statement, and the Macro-Economic Framework Statement.

|---|---|
| **PMJDY** (Jan Dhan Yojana) | Financial inclusion — bank accounts |
| **PM-KISAN** | ₹6,000/year to farmers |
| **PM Ujjwala Yojana** | LPG connections to women |
| **PM Awas Yojana** (Urban/Gramin) | Housing for all |
| **PM Gati Shakti** | National Master Plan for logistics |
| **Production Linked Incentive (PLI)** | Manufacturing |

| **India International Water Festival / Jal Jeevan Mission** | Drinking water |
| **PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana** | Rooftop solar |
| **PM Vishwakarma** | Artisans |

**Committees and councils:**
- **NITI Aayog** (National Institution for Transforming India) — replaced the Planning
  Commission in **2015**.
- **Finance Commission** — chaired by the Finance Minister; the 15th Finance Commission's
  tax devolution formula runs to **41%** of the divisible pool.
- **GST Council** — chaired by the Union Finance Minister; decisions need a **3/4
  majority** of weighted votes.
- **Elections** — the Election Commission of India is a **constitutional body** under
  **Article 324**.
- **CAG** (Comptroller and Auditor General) — Article 148.

**Institutes:**
- **RBI** (1935), **SEBI** (1988), **IRDAI** (1999), **NABARD** (1982), **SIDBI** (1990),
  **EXIM Bank** (1982), **NHB** (1988), **PFRDA** (2003), **NCERT** (1961), **NITI Aayog**
  (2015), **UIDAI** (2009), **CPCB** (1974), **Bureau of Energy Efficiency** (2002),
  **FEMA** (1999), **NSIC**, **FSSAI** (2011), **FSSAI's food safety display**,
  **CERT-In** (2004).
- **SEBI was given statutory powers in 1992**; **IRDAI** in 1999; **PFRDA** in 2003.
- **The Finance Commission** was constituted in **1951**.
- **The Planning Commission** was set up in **1950**.

## Layer 3 — the current-affairs system

**This is 15–30 marks, and it cannot be revised in the last week. It has to be built
over months.**

### The system

**One page per month. One line per item. Nothing else.**

```
NOV 2026
- RBI: MPC kept repo at 6.25% (3 Dec)
- SEBI: revised mutual fund liquidity norms
- Budget 2027: fiscal deficit target 4.2%
- Cabinet approved ₹X lakh crore for recapitalisation of public banks
- New RBI circular: digital lending cooling-off period
- Awards: ___, ___
- Sports: ___
```

**Read 20 minutes a day.** Sources:

| Source | Why |
|---|---|
| **The Hindu** — Business page | RBI, economy, policy, editorial |
| **Indian Express** — Explained / Business | Context and analysis |
| **Business Standard** or **Mint** | Markets, banking, corporate |
| **PIB (press information bureau)** | Government announcements |
| **RBI website** — press releases and circulars | Directly what is asked |
| **SEBI, IRDAI** | Regulatory news |

**Skip** the general news app. You do not need national or international current affairs
for this exam — you need banking, economy and regulatory news.

### What actually gets asked

| Type | Example | Where to get it |
|---|---|---|
| **RBI rate decisions** | "The MPC kept the repo rate unchanged at X%" | RBI press release |
| **RBI circulars** | New norms on digital lending, KYC, penalties | RBI website — read the "What has changed" |
| **Monetary policy** | New MPC members, inflation target | RBI |
| **Government schemes** | Launch, budget allocation, coverage | PIB, Budget documents |
| **Reports and indices** | Economic Survey, Fiscal Policy Statement, Global Hunger Index, World Bank rankings | The report itself |
| **Appointments** | RBI Governor, SEBI chairperson, Finance Commission chair | PIB |
| **Awards** | Bharat Ratna, Padma awards, Nobel, Booker | PIB |
| **Banking awareness** | New account types, RBI instructions | RBI circulars |
| **International** | IMF/World Bank data, G20, BRICS | Follow the Business page |

### The static GK base

Current affairs is half the section; the other half is **static** and is fully
learnable:

- **RBI history and structure** — establishment, nationalisation, governors, committees.
- **Financial institutions** — the DFIs, their founding years, their mandates.
- **Committees** — the ones with famous names (Narasimham, Nachiket Mor, Raghuram Rajan
  on the financial system, Kelkar, N.K. Singh on fiscal policy).
- **Five-year plans** — the National Development Council, the NITI Aayog replacing it.
- **Important days** — National Banking Day (1 October), International Money Fund Day
  (1 October), Financial Literacy Day, etc.
- **Awards** — Padma awards, Param Vir Chakra, Bharat Ratna, Nobel categories.
- **Firsts in banking** — first ATM, first UPI transaction, the first woman RBI Governor
  (Chandra R. Khanwal).

**Build a one-page static GK sheet** and revise it weekly.

### The 20-minute daily routine

| Minutes | What |
|---:|---|
| 5 | Scan the business page headlines — nothing deeper |
| 10 | Add one line per relevant item to the monthly tracker |
| 5 | Revise the previous 7 days' tracker entries from memory |

**The revision step is the whole system.** Reading without revising produces a false
sense of coverage.

## Exam traps by topic

| Topic | Trap |
|---|---|
| Accounts | NRE is tax-free; NRO is taxable; FCNR holds foreign currency |
| ATM/transaction limits | Free UPI transactions is 24 hours, not 10 |
| Deposit insurance | DICGC covers up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank |
| Inflation indices | CPI by NSO/MoSPI; WPI by DPIIT — people swap them |
| MPC | Six members, bi-monthly meetings, 4% target with a ±2% band |
| Inflation target | It is **CPI**, not WPI — a very common trap |
| Repo vs reverse repo | Reverse repo absorbs surplus liquidity |
| CRR vs SLR | CRR is cash with the RBI and not remunerative; SLR is gold/securities and remunerative |
| SEBI | Formed 1988, statutory since 1992 |
| DICGC | ₹5 lakh, per depositor per bank |
| MUDRA tiers | Shishu ₹50,000; Kishore ₹5 lakh; Tarun ₹10 lakh |
| Budget date | 1 February; Economic Survey 31 January |
| GST Council | 3/4 weighted majority |
| NITI Aayog | Replaced the Planning Commission in 2015 |
| Elections | Article 324 |
| NFRA | The National Financial Reporting Authority — independent of the CAG |
